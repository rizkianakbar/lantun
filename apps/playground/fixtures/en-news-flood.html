Coastal towns brace for second week of tidal flooding

By Hannah Osei, Environment correspondent28 Oct 2026, 08:15Updated 2 hours ago

Residents of several coastal towns were told on Wednesday to prepare for another week of flooding, as a combination of spring tides and strong onshore winds pushed seawater over defences for the sixth day in a row.

The Environment Agency said 14 flood warnings remained in place along a 40-mile stretch of coast, and that water levels at high tide on Thursday could exceed those seen at the weekend.

"We have never had it this bad for this long," said Margaret Hale, 71, who has lived on Harbour Road for more than four decades. "You clear up, you go to bed, and the next morning it is back in the kitchen."

The local council has opened three rest centres and is offering free sandbags from the town hall car park. Officials urged people not to drive through floodwater, after two cars were swept off the promenade road on Monday night. Nobody was hurt.

Scientists say the flooding is consistent with long-term sea-level rise, which makes ordinary storm surges more damaging than they were a generation ago. Dr Ian Ferris, a coastal engineer at the University of Southampton, said the defences in the area were "designed for a sea that no longer exists."

The government said a review of coastal funding would report early next year. Until then, residents say they will keep watching the tide tables.
