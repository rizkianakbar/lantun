How to Make a Sourdough Starter From Scratch

Posted on March 2, 2026 by Mara Lindqvist · 6 min read

A sourdough starter is nothing more than flour and water that has been left alone long enough for wild yeast and bacteria to move in. It sounds unlikely, but give it a week and a warm corner of the kitchen, and you will have a bubbling jar that can raise a loaf of bread on its own.

Contents
  1. What you need
  2. Day by day
  3. Troubleshooting

What you need

You need a clean glass jar, a spoon, a kitchen scale, and two flours: whole wheat for the first few days, and plain white flour after that. Whole grain flour carries more of the microbes you are trying to attract, so it gets things going faster.

  • 50 g whole wheat flour
  • 50 g lukewarm water, about 26 °C
  • Patience, roughly seven days of it

Day by day

Day one. Mix the flour and water until no dry bits remain. Scrape down the sides, cover the jar loosely, and leave it somewhere around 24 °C. Nothing visible will happen yet, and that is fine.

Days two and three. You may see a few bubbles, or a sudden burst of activity followed by silence. That early rise comes from bacteria that do not last, so do not be fooled. Discard all but 50 g and feed it with 50 g of flour and 50 g of water.
Keep the same time each day if you can.

Days four to seven. Switch to white flour. Feed it twice a day. When the starter reliably doubles within six hours of a feed, it is ready. Mr. Okafor, a baker in Leeds who tested this method with me, called it "boring in the best way," and I think that is exactly right.

The starter does not care about your schedule. Learn to read the jar instead of the clock.

Troubleshooting

If a grey liquid collects on top, your starter is hungry. Pour it off and feed it. If the jar smells like nail polish remover, feed it more often. And if nothing happens at all after a week, check the temperature; a cold kitchen slows everything down by days, not hours.

Once it is active, you can keep the starter in the fridge and feed it once a week. Take it out the night before you bake, give it a feed, and it will be ready by morning.
