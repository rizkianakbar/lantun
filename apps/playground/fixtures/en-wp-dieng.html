Three Cold Mornings on the Dieng Plateau

July 14, 2026 · Indonesia, Central Java

Nobody tells you how cold Java can get. At two thousand metres above sea level, the Dieng Plateau wakes up to frost on the potato fields in the dry season, and the steam rising from the craters looks almost theatrical against the blue morning sky.

I spent three days there in July, sleeping in a family guesthouse in Dieng Kulon and waking at four every morning to catch the sunrise. Here is what I learned, including the mistakes.

Day one: Sikunir at dawn

The walk up Sikunir hill takes about thirty minutes from the car park at Sembungan village. It is steep but well paved, and you will not be alone: by 4:45 a.m. the path was a slow river of headlamps. At the top, the sun came up behind Mount Sindoro, and for about ten minutes everyone went quiet.

Bring gloves. I did not, and I could not work my camera for the first half of the sunrise.

Day two: craters and a coloured lake

Kawah Sikidang is the easiest crater to visit, and also the smelliest. Boardwalks keep you away from the boiling mud, and local vendors sell boiled eggs cooked in the vents. Telaga Warna, a short drive away, shifts from green to turquoise depending on the light, because of the sulphur in the water.

The temples of the Arjuna complex are some of the oldest in Java, built in the 7th or 8th century. They are small, and that is part of their charm: you can walk around all five in under an hour, often with no one else around.

Day three: the food

Try mie ongklok, a local noodle dish served in a thick, starchy broth with satay on the side. Also try carica, a small mountain papaya that only grows at this altitude. It comes preserved in syrup in little jars, and it makes a good gift.

I left on the third afternoon with cold feet, a full stomach, and a promise to come back in August for the Dieng Culture Festival.
